## Main teaching points

- `OIL-01`: establish the routine baseline; licensed service-provider context; compliant close-proximity transfer
- `OIL-02`: avoid over-triggering on proximity alone; require multi-layer evidence
- `OIL-03`: practise disciplined ambiguity; thin paperwork; hotspot context without proof
- `OIL-04`: recognise coastal-state enforcement as a major signal; separate confirmed facts from suspected origin stories
- `OIL-05`: combine sanctions identity, STS activity, and AIS disablement into a high-risk cluster
- `OIL-06`: understand proliferation-relevant refined-petroleum STS and advisory-listed vessel risk
- `COAL-01`: distinguish routine Indonesian offshore coal logistics from covert tanker STS
- `COAL-02`: recognise a coal-specific sanctions-evasion typology; lightering; certificate-of-origin risk

## Strong anchors not selected as separate cases

- OFAC 2025 Iran shipping advisory as a standalone card
  Reason: strong backbone for facilitator logic, but it is guidance rather than one clean encounter and works better as supporting provenance across several oil cases.

- The unconfirmed Iranian-origin claim around the Penang interdiction
  Reason: too messy for participant-facing use because the research pack itself treats cargo origin as unconfirmed in public reporting.

- Additional Gulf of Tonkin vessels from the UN coal set (`KA RIM CHON`, `PHO PHYONG`, `TAE YANG`)
  Reason: strong anchors, but they repeat the same lightering typology already captured by `COAL-02`.

- Separate benign and concerning variants of the Malaysia/Singapore outer-limits grey-zone case
  Reason: evidence is intentionally thin, and splitting it into multiple cards would make the set repetitive without adding enough new workshop value.
